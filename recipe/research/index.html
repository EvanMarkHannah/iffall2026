Spanakopita Triangles


Original recipe by Yumna Jawad

These homemade Greek spanakopita triangles are a delicious appetizer bursting with flavor that can be likened to Middle Eastern spinach pies. Phyllo (filo) pastry is filled with seasoned spinach and feta cheese for a delicious vegetarian snack. You’ll be sure to impress your guests with these!

  • Prep Time: 30 minutes
  • Cook Time: 25 minutes
  • Servings: 40

Ingredients:

  • 16 ounces frozen spinach defrosted, drained
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 1 medium red onion diced
  • 2 cloves garlic minced
  • 2 teaspoons dried dill
  • ½ cup parsley leaves finely chopped and loosely packed
  • ¼ cup green onions finely chopped, about 5 – white and light green portions
  • 2 teaspoon lemon zest
  • ½ teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 ½ cup crumbled feta cheese about 6 oz
  • 6 tablespoons unsalted butter melted
  • About 20 sheets one sleeve phyllo pastry, defrosted

Instructions:

Make the filling:

  1. Heat olive oil in a large skillet over medium heat. Add the onions and cook, stirring regularly, until it starts to soften, about 5 minutes
  2. Add the garlic and dill. Cook with the onions, stirring frequently, until caramelized and soft, about 5 more minutes. Set aside and allow to cool.
  3. Squeeze the defrosted spinach to drain all the water possible. You should end up with around 1 cup of spinach. Transfer to the skillet on top of the onion mixture.
  4. Add the parsley, green onions, lemon zest, ground pepper, feta cheese to the skillet and stir to distribute mixture evenly.

Assemble the triangles:

  1. Preheat the oven to 375° F. Line a baking sheet with parchment.
  2. On a clean surface, roll out the phyllo pastry. Cut into 2 ½-3” wide strips lengthwise using a pizza cutter. Cover the pastry with a barely damp towel to prevent it from drying out.
  3. On a clean surface, layout 1 layer of phyllo pastry and brush it down with a small amount of butter using a pastry brush and very gentle motions.
  4. Place a second layer of pastry directly on top of the first and brush that with a small amount of butter.
  5. In one corner of the pastry layers, place 1 heaping tablespoon of filling mixture. Squish it into an approximate triangle that nearly reaches the edges.
  6. Fold the corner of the pasty with the filling over to reach the nearest opposite edge of the pastry. Then, roll the filling triangle towards the empty end of the pastry. Again, fold the triangle to the nearest opposite side. Repeat these last two steps until you reach the end of the pastry rectangle.
  7. Place the triangle on the prepared baking sheet and cover with a damp towel. Repeat the assembly with all the pastry sheets and filling. The triangles can be placed fairly close together on the baking sheet, about ½”.
  8. Bake for 20-25 minutes until golden brown and crisp.
  9. Remove from the oven and allow to cool for 10 minutes before serving.

thedomesticgeek.com

This site has a simple, easy to understannd design with clear sections. It gets straight to the recipe and doesn't meander like others do. I like how the description, yeild, and time estimates are displayed, and the easy to use "print" button seems like a useful feature. Unfortunatly it contains pretty much no pictures, which is kind of a problem for this recipe since folding the triangles is difficult to understand without a visual guide.

dinneratthezoo.com

While the layout of this page leaves a little to be desired compared to the previous one, it makes up for its sprawling inefficency by being much more in depth and containing tons of pictures. This site contains an automatic doubling and tripling button for the ingredient amounts, which could come in handy. Perhaps one where you can input any number would be fun. This recipe conatins much more detail about what spanakopita is, including what course it is served in. It also lists nutrition facts, alternative fillings/flavors to try, and helpful tips about cooking and storing the food.

allrecipes.com

Finally, this site is probably the worst of the three. While it contains many of the useful features of the others, like an ingredient multiplier and nutrition facts, the quality of the instructions is severely lacking. There are no progress pictures and the recipe consists of only four steps, compared to the others' 8-10. I would probably struggle to use this recipe as it does not describe what I need to do in enough detail. Two redeeming qualities that the other sites lack is a switch that can be clicked to stop the screen from falling asleep while you are cooking and looking at the recipe and a much more robust review and social aspect. There are many user-submitted photos of their finished spanakopita and lots of reviews. This might help users more accurately gadge the realistic output of this recipe.

While the bare asethetics of this site seem irrelevant to this project, there are several basic things I really enjoy. The same effect of changing the screen with cursor movement continues through the whole part of the site, which helps it feel less gimmicky.

marina-zakharova.netlify.app

This is an attempt at a tactile website experience that falls short where the other two don't. While the opening is cool, making you wipe away a pink mask with your cursor to see the home page, it goes downhill from there. The prior two sites were very careful in subtly indicating to the user which elements are interactive/clickable and which are not. This site fails to intuitively diffrentatie between the two through design, leading to a more frustrating and confusing experience. Many of the prominent elements on the home page practicly bed you to click them, moving excitedly with animated arrows pointing at them, however they are not clickable. Similarly, the subheading contains rectangualr shapes with words inside, with one rectangle's text and background colors inverted. This is a common way to re-color buttons when a user's cursor hovers over them to show that they are clickable, which gives the impression that these rectangular elements, centered below the heading, on the home page, are indeed buttons. However, they are not. To see the real buttons, one must look at the very top of the screen where the small, practically transparent buttons are, shown in a completely different style to the rest of the site. They feel jarring and out of place, and worst of all, make for a completely unintuitve user experience.
